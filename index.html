Thesis: prioritize, package, scale

Governments drive a large share of payment flows, and enabler partners already sit inside the agencies that move this money.

1

Prioritize

Build one ranked pipeline of partners, use cases and markets with the in-market VGS sales leads, so that each partner has a clear reason to work with Visa and a named first opportunity.

2

Package

Turn each priority use case into a joint solution that combines Visa capabilities with the partner platform, and feed the gaps back to the Visa solutions teams for the product roadmap.

3

Scale

Own the partners that already operate in several Asia Pacific markets with account plans, contracting, onboarding and joint RFP strategies, so that one agreement opens several markets.

1

National instant and QR rails grow and connect across borders

Signal

QRIS reached 45.46 million merchants by July 2026, and it went live with China's QR system on 30 April 2026. ASEAN cross-border QR payments reached 12.9 million transactions in the first half of 2025. The BIS handed Project Nexus over to Nexus Global Payments.

For VGS

Government payouts and domestic collections move to account-to-account rails such as PayNow and GovWallet. Visa wins where it adds cross-border reach, spend controls, credit, tokens and data on top of these rails.

DisbursementsCollections
2

Sovereignty and regulation reshape card economics

Signal

In Australia, eftpos, Mastercard and Visa introduced no-surcharge rules from 1 October 2026, and the RBA lowered interchange caps and added a cap for foreign cards. In Indonesia, national network rules for GPN and QRIS remain part of US and Indonesian trade discussions.

For VGS

A government is a regulator and a customer at the same time. Acceptance contracts with agencies that used to surcharge can change, and close work with Government Engagement keeps sales and policy aligned.

AcceptancePolicy
3

Agentic commerce moves from concept to pilots

Signal

Visa launched Agentic Ready in Asia Pacific on 30 April 2026 with partners from 10 markets, including 13 issuers in Singapore. DBS was the first issuer in Asia Pacific to pilot Visa Intelligent Commerce, in February 2026.

For VGS

Procurement and expense workflows are a natural government use case, where agents buy inside purchasing-card controls. This is my own hypothesis to test with the team.

CommercialAI
4

Stablecoins and tokenized settlement reach production

Signal

Visa's stablecoin settlement pilot supports nine blockchains. On 18 November 2026, Visa named Singapore-based dtcpay as the Asia Pacific pilot partner for stablecoin prefunding of Visa Direct payouts. MAS BLOOM builds the policy frame in Singapore.

For VGS

This applies to cross-border government flows such as aid, scholarships and overseas payouts. Governments move slowly on digital assets, so this is a medium-term topic.

DisbursementsNew flows
5

Money movement, commercial payments and services lead growth

Signal

In FY2026, Visa value-added services revenue grew 30% in constant dollars to $14 billion, commercial payments volume grew 12%, and Visa Direct passed 15 billion transactions. These are global figures.

For VGS

The VGS families sit in these growth areas: disbursements run on Visa Direct, purchasing and travel cards are commercial payments, and data, risk and controls are value-added services.

All families
6

Scams test citizen trust in digital channels

Signal

Singapore's scam situation improved in the first half of 2026, but investment scams caused the highest losses, and MAS will not extend the Shared Responsibility Framework to transfers that victims authorize. Scammers also impersonate government officials and banks. Visa names identity as the frontline of security for 2026.

For VGS

Citizens must trust the channels they use to pay and receive money from government. Tokenized credentials and strong authentication protect the citizen at the moment of payment, and this supports adoption of digital government services.

Citizen trustAuthentication
7

Cross-border travel stays strong

Signal

In FY2026, Visa cross-border volume excluding intra-Europe rose 12%, with travel up 11% and e-commerce up 15%.

For VGS

Visitor spending is a government KPI, so tourism boards buy data and acceptance. This explains the Visa agreements with tourism bodies in Singapore, Thailand and the Philippines.

Data insightsTourism
8

Agencies buy risk and identity tools, beyond the payment

Signal

At the Visa Digital Government Forum in Vietnam (March 2026), Visa presented government use cases in three areas: welfare and pensions (benefit claims, entitlement checks), revenue and taxation (tax and contract compliance) and citizen wallets and digital identity (onboarding, attribute verification). The fraud types include synthetic IDs, duplicate claims, procurement fraud, deepfakes and SIM swaps.

For VGS

This is program integrity for the agency, before any money moves. Visa can add value even where a national rail carries the payout. The route to market is through the integrators that run welfare and tax systems and through identity and DPI providers.

Value-added servicesPartners
In one sentence: national rails take more domestic flows, money movement and commercial payments grow faster than consumer cards, and new rails such as agents and stablecoins need trust, so the partner role is to bring banks, integrators and fintechs that design Visa into government programs early.

RFPs and deal structuring

Agencies usually buy payment services from a bank, an acquirer or an integrator. Visa enters the RFP through the specification (brand, credential, data, controls) or through the bidder it supports.

Anatomy of a government payments RFP

1. Market engagementThe agency runs an RFI or market sounding to test options and shape the scope.Partner move: shape requirements early, inside procurement rules
2. RFP or panel issuedThe scope is set as one contract, a panel or a framework with lots that agencies call off.Partner move: bid or no-bid, and choose the lead bidder
3. RequirementsFunctional scope, card or acceptance products, integration with finance and travel systems, security, data and reporting.Partner move: map each requirement to Visa and partner capability
4. Pricing scheduleFees to the agency, fees to cardholders or citizens, rebates and any cost-to-State limits.Partner move: joint pricing logic and a value case
5. Evaluation and awardScored on price, capability, references and risk, often with presentations and clarifications.Partner move: references, case studies and demos
6. Contract and mobilizationA multi-year term with extension options, service levels and onboarding of agencies.Partner move: launch plan, adoption and spend growth

Pattern: the bank, acquirer or integrator holds the contract, while Visa shapes the specification and strengthens the bidder. This is why the choice of partner before the RFP matters so much.

How VGS supports partner-led bids

Partner-led RFP support

  • Bid or no-bid review with the partner and the in-market sales lead
  • Clear roles for Visa, the partner and the issuing or acquiring bank in the response
  • Visa solution content, security and compliance answers, and case studies
  • Joint pricing logic and a value case for the agency, such as cost to collect or time to disburse
  • Early engagement with the agency through the partner, inside procurement rules
  • Post-award plan for launch, onboarding and adoption

Commercial and contracting frameworks

  • Referral or reseller agreement with a revenue share on volume
  • Co-investment in go-to-market, such as joint events, pilots and marketing funds
  • Technology partnership with API integration and service level commitments
  • Program enrollment, such as Visa Ready, with certification steps
  • Strategic agreement for multi-market partners with an annual joint business plan
  • Review and exit clauses tied to the partner scorecard

First 90 days, proposed

A plan to test with the team, built around the role scope.

Days 1 to 30: listen and map

Meet the in-market VGS sales leads, the solutions teams and global VGS colleagues. Review the signed partners and the live RFPs. Build the first ranked list of partners, use cases and markets.

Days 31 to 60: prioritize and package

Agree the top partners per market with the sales leads. Write the joint value propositions for two or three priority use cases, and open structured discussions with the first multi-market partners.

Days 61 to 90: prove it in the field

Start joint account plans with the priority partners, support the first joint RFP response, and set a quarterly review cadence with partner scorecards for leadership.
